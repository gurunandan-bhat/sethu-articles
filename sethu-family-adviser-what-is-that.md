 Author: Rebecca Manari | May 16th, 2022

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large">![](https://i0.wp.com/sethu.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/rebecca-copy-1024x1024.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024)</figure>I’m Rebecca Manari and I am on the Sethu Team as a Family Adviser. I’ve been a teacher and writer for about 15 years, so this is a slightly different role for me, but one that suits me very well. My three-year-old son is on the autism spectrum and we have been receiving support from Sethu’s expert professionals for the past 14 months. He has come a long way since our first session at Sethu and I have also learned so much.

A Family Adviser is a parent who uses their own experiences and ideas to discuss decisions and help shape programmes and policies for other parents.

Some of my work involves getting feedback from families about the support they receive from Sethu, being a bridge between the professionals and the parents. I also assist in making sure parents understand what else is available to their children and families, at Sethu. Working together towards reasonable solutions helps eliminate some stress and am around if explanations or support is needed. Helping parents connect via a support group and encouraging them to support each other is also part of the role. I make sure I am up to date so I can provide information to families about other facilities; support and benefits that are available to them.

I put forward suggestions on how Sethu can further help with the feedback I receive from parents. I also conduct regular webinars full of information on ASD for parents of newly diagnosed children. It can be overwhelming to receive and digest a diagnosis so these confidential sessions fill a void and offer support.

What I love about the work I do is that I enjoy talking to other people with similar challenges as mine, and working together on positive solutions for all of our children. Supporting families just like mine is very fulfilling and satisfying. I feel like I can relate well to what other parents are experiencing with their children. Having been through many of the same experiences makes my job easy too!
